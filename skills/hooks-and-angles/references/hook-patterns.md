# Hook Patterns Reference

## The One Rule
Hooks must grab attention in the FIRST SECOND. By second 2, they've either stopped or scrolled past.

## 7 Core Hook Patterns

### 1. Time + Action
Structure: [Timeframe] + [specific action/result]
Examples:
- "In 30 days I cut my ad spend in half"
- "Last week I fired my entire creative team"
- "3 months ago I couldn't get a single profitable ad"

### 2. Unexpected Truth
Structure: [Common belief] + [surprising reversal]
Examples:
- "The best-performing ad I ever ran was ugly"
- "Your highest ROAS campaign is probably losing money"
- "More ad spend doesn't mean more sales"

### 3. Personal Stakes
Structure: [I/We] + [high-stakes situation]
Examples:
- "I almost shut down my agency over one campaign"
- "We burned $50K before finding what actually works"
- "My biggest client nearly fired me last month"

### 4. Contrarian Take
Structure: Challenge an industry assumption directly
Examples:
- "Stop split testing your hooks"
- "Broad targeting beats interest targeting every time"
- "Your creative agency is testing wrong"

### 5. Problem Compression
Structure: Compress a complex problem into one sharp line
Examples:
- "90% of your ads will fail. Here's what to do about it."
- "You're optimizing for the wrong metric"
- "The ad that converts isn't the ad that gets likes"

### 6. Numbers + Contrast
Structure: [Specific number] + [unexpected comparison]
Examples:
- "3% yield beats 6% yield over time"
- "$5 leads vs $20 leads — same number of sales"
- "1 winning ad pays for 9 losers"

### 7. Insider Detail
Structure: Reveal something only an expert would know
Examples:
- "The Facebook API doesn't track sales after 28 days"
- "Duplicating a campaign resets the learning phase entirely"
- "Social proof on ads compounds like interest"

## Hook Rules
1. First second matters most — instant visual impact + instant relevance
2. Be specific to ONE micro-avatar (generic = ignored, specific = personal)
3. Pattern interrupt — break expectations
4. Create a curiosity gap — make them need the next line
5. Use their language — words they actually say, not marketing speak

## What Kills a Hook
- Starting with a question (passive, easy to ignore)
- Generic claims ("Want better results?")
- Long setup before the punch
- Industry jargon the audience doesn't use
- Trying to be clever instead of clear
