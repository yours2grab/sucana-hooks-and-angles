# Direct Response Techniques Reference

## Core Frameworks for Ad Scripts

### PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solve
The most reliable framework for cold traffic.

1. **Problem:** Name their specific pain (use their words)
2. **Agitate:** Make it worse — show consequences of NOT solving it
3. **Solve:** Introduce your solution as the natural answer

**Best for:** Problem-aware and solution-aware audiences
**Script length:** Works at any length (15s to 90s)

**Example structure:**
- Problem (0-5s): "Your Meta ads are bleeding money every day"
- Agitate (5-15s): "Every $100 you spend, $90 goes to ads that will never convert. That's not a bad month — that's how ads work. The question is whether your winners make up for it."
- Solve (15-30s): "Here's how the top 10% of your ads can carry the other 90%..."

---

### BAB — Before, After, Bridge
Paints a transformation picture.

1. **Before:** Their current painful state
2. **After:** The desired state they want
3. **Bridge:** Your product/method connects the two

**Best for:** Solution-aware audiences who need to see the outcome
**Script length:** Works best at 30s+

---

### AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action
Classic direct response flow.

1. **Attention:** Hook that stops the scroll
2. **Interest:** Relevant information that keeps them watching
3. **Desire:** Benefits and proof that make them want it
4. **Action:** Clear CTA

**Best for:** Product-aware audiences. They know solutions exist.
**Script length:** 30s-90s (needs time for all four stages)

---

### Star-Story-Solution
Uses narrative to sell.

1. **Star:** Introduce a character (the customer, the founder, a case study)
2. **Story:** Tell their struggle (relatable, specific)
3. **Solution:** How the product changed their outcome

**Best for:** UGC-style ads, testimonial formats
**Script length:** 30s-90s

---

### 4 U's — Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific, Useful
Primarily for hooks and headlines, but structures entire short ads.

Every element must be:
- **Urgent:** Why now, not later
- **Unique:** Why this, not alternatives
- **Ultra-specific:** Exact numbers, names, details
- **Useful:** Clear practical value

**Best for:** Short-form ads (15s-30s), especially hooks
**Script length:** 15s-30s

---

## VSL (Video Sales Letter) Structure

The standard long-form video ad structure:

| Section | Duration | Purpose |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Hook | 0-3s | Stop the scroll. Pattern interrupt. |
| Problem | 3-15s | Name and agitate the pain. Use their language. |
| Credibility | 15-25s | Why should they listen? Data, credentials, results. |
| Solution | 25-40s | Introduce the product/method. Focus on mechanism. |
| Proof | 40-55s | Testimonials, case studies, numbers. Stack evidence. |
| CTA | 55-60s+ | Clear next step. Remove risk. Create urgency. |

**Timing adapts to script length:**
- 15s: Hook (2s) + Problem (5s) + Solution (5s) + CTA (3s)
- 30s: Hook (3s) + Problem (8s) + Solution (10s) + Proof (5s) + CTA (4s)
- 60s: Full structure above
- 90s: Extended proof section, multiple testimonials, deeper mechanism explanation

## The Testing Hierarchy

When testing ad variations, test in this order:

1. **ANGLE** (most important) — The core message. What are you saying?
2. **HOOK** (second) — How you grab attention for that message
3. **FORMAT** (third) — Video, image, carousel, UGC, talking head

"The most important thing is the message. Within that we can say that message a thousand ways."

Test angles first. Once you find a winning angle, test hooks within it. Once you find a winning hook, test formats.

## The 90% Reality

90% of ads will fail. This is normal. The 10% of winners must be profitable enough to cover the losses of the other 90%. This means:
- Test volume matters more than perfection
- Kill losers fast (3x target CPA, no conversions)
- Scale winners aggressively (duplicate, increase budget)
- Never stop testing new angles — winners fatigue
